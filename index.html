The Experiment

Each attacker generates a sequence of n independent and identically distributed penetration-score increments. The simulation repeats this process for m attackers, producing m sample trajectories. The score is an abstract mathematical measure rather than a calibrated real-world probability of compromising a system.

Variance Analysis

Total-score variance grows linearly with n. Average-score variance decreases as 1/n, while √n normalization keeps the variance constant. The chart uses separate vertical scales to make these different behaviors visible.

Systems and Attackers

Increasing n extends each trajectory and reduces the variance of its average. Increasing m does not change the theoretical score distribution of an individual attacker; it provides more independent observations, improving the empirical estimate of that distribution.

MATHEMATICAL INTERPRETATION

What the simulations demonstrate

01

Convergence in Probability

The average penetration score approaches its theoretical expectation as n grows.

02

Variance and Scaling

Normalizing by n reduces variability, whereas normalizing by √n preserves a constant variance.

03

Convergence in Distribution

In the symmetric Rademacher model, normalized sums approach a standard normal distribution.

For Bernoulli increments, the corresponding standard-normal CLT uses (Sₙ − np)/√(np(1−p)), rather than Sₙ/√n. All simulations assume independent, identically distributed increments.
